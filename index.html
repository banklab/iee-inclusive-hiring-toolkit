AEvaluation criteria & scoring

Used at every stage

  • A written score sheet (adapted from a University of Michigan template) rates scientific contribution, service, potential to enrich the institute's community, contribution to diversity, equity and inclusion, teaching and mentoring.
  • An explicit “add for the IEE” prompt asks which perspective, competence or experience a candidate would bring that the institute does not yet have. Evaluators also note career barriers a candidate may have faced.
  • Judgements are qualitative. No journal-based or other quantitative metrics are used, in line with DORA, CoARA and the Better Science Initiative. Academic age and unequal starting opportunities are taken into account.

BDiversity-aware sourcing

Before the application deadline

  • Before the deadline, we actively scouted potential candidates from underrepresented groups and recorded them in a shared list. Deliberate outreach broadened the pool, instead of leaving it to whoever happened to apply.
  • Around 20 candidates were approached across the two searches.

CPrivilege-aware initial review

First review round only

  • We extended the “Lausanne model”, in which women and men are first reviewed in separate groups, with a third group: candidates who grew up with comparatively few academic and economic opportunities.
  • This group is defined transparently from Human Development Index scores (1990) and research capacity (PhD-level researchers per million inhabitants).
  • The grouping applies only in the first round. There are no quotas on the shortlist or in any later step. The committee also pledges to attend to intersecting dimensions such as disability, racialisation and LGBTQ+ identity.

DBias-aware committee process

Individual review, discussion and shortlist

  • Each 13-person committee includes professors (1–2 from other institutes), Mittelbau members and early-career researchers, all with equal votes.
  • Every member completes two half-days of DEI / anti-bias training and an orientation on process, confidentiality and data protection.
  • Conflicts of interest are declared on a five-level scale, and the closest ties exclude a member from that candidate. Each application is read by three people, including at least one professor and one early-career researcher.
  • A trained, neutral moderator leads the all-day discussion. Speaking order runs from the lowest to the highest conflict-of-interest level and career stage.

EStructured interviews

Two-day on-site visit

  • Every candidate completes the same standardised teaching assignment (see F3).
  • Interviewers use a pre-agreed set of questions, identical for all candidates within a search, and a structured evaluation tool. Judgements then rest on consistent evidence rather than gut feeling.

FInclusive candidate experience

From invitation to feedback

  • Candidates get early, transparent information about format, expectations and logistics. They are explicitly invited to share any needs: accessibility, dietary requirements, prayer or lactation rooms, all-gender toilets.
  • Name tags show names and roles. A welcome pack gives directions, and candidates can view the interview rooms beforehand. Travel, accommodation and visa costs are covered, and international candidates are met on arrival.
  • The audience and committees are reminded of common biases and asked to engage with every candidate. All institute members can feed back to the committee through their status group.

Using the toolkit

What we learned, and how to make it your own.

What we would refine

  • The review grouping simplifies a messy reality. It foregrounds gender and economic opportunity, so racialisation, disability and their intersections have to be carried by training and conscious discussion.
  • Recording candidates' career barriers consistently.
  • Keeping DEI awareness fresh over a months-long process.
  • Communicating decisions transparently to the many strong applicants who are not shortlisted.

Adapting it

  • Each document stands on its own. Start with the measure that fits your next search.
  • The score sheet and interview questions are easy to adjust for other disciplines.
  • We are glad to share our experience, including what we would do differently, and to compare notes. Contact Prof. Dr. Claudia Bank, Executive Director of the IEE: claudia.bank[@]unibe.ch.
